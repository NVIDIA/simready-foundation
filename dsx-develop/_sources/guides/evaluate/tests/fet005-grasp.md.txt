# FET005 Grasp

Tests in this family evaluate an asset with a generated gripper against
configured lift, hold, shake and release-motion criteria.

## Overview

This family places a standardized parallel-jaw gripper at each declared grasp point on the asset, closes the jaws onto the asset under real gravity, and lifts the gripper to confirm that the asset follows the motion. The gripper then holds the asset in the air, applies a circular horizontal orbit to stress the grip, and finally opens to release. The test uses the active configured physics runtime.

A grasp identifier may be parented directly below its rigid body or placed in
a sibling annotation scope below the asset default prim. The runtime resolves
the body crossed by the authored grasp segment and uses that same body for
settling, fixture sizing, and motion tracking.

Identifiers support the GSP.001 direct `BasisCurves` form named
`grasp_identifier*`, as well as the legacy named `Xform` wrapper with a child
points-based line. The selected source's first two authored points are
transformed through its complete composed world transform, including scale.
A named curve already consumed by its legacy wrapper is not run twice.
No segment is inferred from the asset bounds or synthesized to make a grasp
possible; unavailable, nonfinite or zero-length segments leave setup incomplete.

## What a Passing Family Means

At least one declared grasp identifier satisfied this configured fixture's
observed lift, hold, shake and release-motion criteria in the selected runtime.
An overall pass does not guarantee that every identifier succeeds, directly
measure contact/holding force, or certify a real-world pick-and-place workflow.
Refer to the per-identifier metrics, video, effective setup and proxy limitations.

## Tests

The standardized gripper is generated by the test and uses runtime-native
finger control. PhysX commands both balanced linear drives; Newton controls
both symmetric jaws with separate native PD actuators. These fixture controls
do not alter the submitted asset.

Setup/control errors and missing required live state are incomplete execution,
not evaluated asset failures. An empty-grasp or release failure requires fixture,
placement and contact investigation before assigning asset ownership. See the
procedure's measurement and release limits.

Completed intermediate and terminal phase dictionaries are retained separately
per identifier, including successful close, lift and release observations.
A diagnostic export failure cannot turn a measured outcome into a different
verdict; absent detail is unavailable evidence, not a failed phase.
